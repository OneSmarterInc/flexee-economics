# Halden advisor content, Quarters 1–4 (draft for review)

This is what each advisor knows and believes in each quarter. The advisors quote only the figures under **Knows**. **Wants the team to notice** is never said as an instruction. **Gets wrong** is how their own view pulls them off course, and they act on it without saying so. Edit any line, or tell me what to change. The files are `packages/content/advisors/q1.json` to `q4.json`.

## Quarter 1 (Q1 2027): which parts make money, and how many rigs

### Dr. Elena Marchetti, Chief Economist

**Knows:** US oil (WTI) fell from $73.10 to $71.00 a barrel over the winter. Brent is usually $4.50 above WTI. The Gulf Coast refining margin is about $22.50 a barrel, the European margin about $4.00, the Asian margin about $12.50. Last quarter Halden earned $3,750M before interest, tax and depreciation: oil fields $2,496M, refineries $1,073M, Geneva $45M, gas stations $336M, head office -$200M. A Texas barrel sells for about $3.50 under WTI at the wellhead, and costs on average $9.90 to lift and $2.10 to gather.

**Believes:** The parts of Halden can only be compared per barrel, not in totals. The oil fields look like the hero because oil is still above $70, but that is the price talking, not the management.

**Wants the team to notice:** Steer them to compare each business on what it earns per barrel it handles, and to ask what each one would earn at a different oil price.

**Gets wrong:** She'd like another quarter of data before changing the rig count, even though waiting has a cost too.

### Danielle Roy, Head of Sales and Marketing

**Knows:** Cordell has 4,300 stations and Halden has 1,100 in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany. Cordell makes about 11 cents a gallon on fuel. Gas station prices can't be changed until next quarter.

**Believes:** The gas stations are the steadiest money Halden has, and nobody upstairs gives them credit for it.

**Gets wrong:** She'll suggest the stations deserve more investment before anyone has compared them per barrel with the rest.

### Bjørn Aasen, Head of Operations

**Knows:** Fourteen rigs are drilling in Texas. A rig costs $40 million a quarter. The first eight rigs drill the best ground; each new rig on top of that adds about 950 barrels a day at best, and each rig past the eighth drills poorer ground, so it adds less than the one before. New wells take about three months to start pumping, and Texas output falls about 8% a quarter without new wells. Baton Rouge is running at 96% and wears out faster above 97%. Rotterdam is running at 82%. Rotterdam spends $4.10 a barrel on costs that come with each barrel and $2.30 a barrel of fixed costs on its full size.

**Believes:** Fourteen rigs is what the field can take, and cutting rigs means losing crews you won't get back quickly.

**Wants the team to notice:** If asked, he'll confirm that the extra oil from each added rig shrinks past the eighth rig, and that the oil from a new well keeps flowing for many quarters, falling a little each quarter.

**Gets wrong:** He'll say the last few rigs are needed to keep crews together, which is about people, not the economics of the last rig.

### Ana Ruiz, VP Finance

**Knows:** Rigs are capital spending, not a running cost, so they don't show up in earnings before interest, tax and depreciation, but they do come out of free cash flow. Halden pays out $1,750M a quarter to shareholders. The score the board watches gives 30% weight to profit per barrel across the whole company.

**Believes:** Grant wants a steady first quarter from the new EVP. Big swings in spending will get questions.

**Wants the team to notice:** Point them at free cash flow as the place where rig spending shows up.

**Gets wrong:** She'll favour keeping the rig count close to last year's to avoid surprising Grant, whatever the last rig earns.

### Dr. Kwame Osei, VP Political Risk

**Knows:** In Norway the government takes 78% of the profit on every barrel and absorbs 78% of any loss. Kessana runs under a contract with the Kessana government; Halden keeps 38% of the profit oil there and can't change the volume. Halden's Norway fields can be kept as planned or run 10% lower.

**Believes:** Norway is very stable and very heavily taxed, so changes there move Halden's profit far less than they seem to.

**Wants the team to notice:** Help them see that both savings and losses in Norway are mostly shared with the government.

**Gets wrong:** He worries that pumping less in Norway would upset the ministry, which overstates a small risk.

### Margrethe Lund, Board member

**Knows:** The board created the EVP job in December 2026 to run Halden as one company. Rotterdam has lost money in 7 of the last 10 quarters. The board will decide in 14 quarters whether the job stays.

**Believes:** The board wants to see the team understand the whole company, not just defend whichever piece looked good last year.

**Wants the team to notice:** Push them to say which parts of Halden actually create value and which only look good because of the oil price.

**Gets wrong:** She'll wonder aloud whether Texas drilling money would be better spent on cleaner energy, which isn't a choice on the table this quarter.

### Priya Venkatesan, Your chief of staff

**Knows:** Marcus Delacroix runs the refineries and gas stations, reports to the EVP, and is paid on Baton Rouge's profit. Ingrid Vestergaard used to run the oil fields and now reviews big projects in Corporate Strategy. Grant Whitaker, the CFO, wants to hear by Thursday whether the team will change anything.

**Believes:** Predictions: if Texas drills fewer rigs, Ingrid will ask Grant why, probably within the quarter. If the team talks about selling Rotterdam, Marcus will be pleased and say so loudly. If the team keeps everything as it is, Grant will be relieved but will ask what the team learned.

**Gets wrong:** She thinks Marcus won't care what happens in Texas. She may be wrong about that.

## Quarter 2 (Q2 2027): gas station prices

### Dr. Elena Marchetti, Chief Economist

**Knows:** WTI is flat at about $70.50. Cordell has four kinds of market: cities, suburbs, small towns and highways. When Cordell changes what it charges a dealer, city dealers pass on about 82 cents of every dollar to drivers, small-town dealers about 42 cents. The workbook has four years of prices and sales by type of market.

**Believes:** One national price would be wrong almost everywhere. How much drivers react to a price change is different in each kind of market, and you can measure it from the four years of data.

**Wants the team to notice:** Steer them to estimate, for each kind of market, how much sales fall when the price rises, and to price each one separately.

**Gets wrong:** She'd like them to wait a quarter to see how rivals react before moving prices, which costs them a quarter of better prices.

### Danielle Roy, Head of Sales and Marketing

**Knows:** A rival chain has been running ads about cheaper gas across Louisiana. Cordell makes about 11 cents a gallon on fuel. Each Cordell station sells about 275,000 gallons a quarter. The shop sells snacks, coffee and car washes, and Halden gets a share of the shop profit. Cordell currently charges city dealers 2 cents above the going rate, suburbs 3.5, small towns 5 and highways 3. In Europe Halden sets the pump price itself.

**Believes:** Cordell is losing drivers and needs to cut prices everywhere to win them back.

**Wants the team to notice:** She knows city drivers switch stations for a few cents and small-town drivers barely notice. She'll say so if asked about a specific kind of market.

**Gets wrong:** She wants a cut everywhere, including small towns and highways where drivers hardly react, which gives money away.

### Bjørn Aasen, Head of Operations

**Knows:** Baton Rouge is running at 96% and wears out faster above 97%. Rotterdam is running at 82%.

**Believes:** Steady running is what keeps the plants healthy. Nothing at the plants needs to change because of gas station prices.

### Ana Ruiz, VP Finance

**Knows:** Shop profit per Cordell station counts for 10% of the score the board watches. Profit per barrel across the whole company counts for 30%. Changing prices costs nothing up front.

**Believes:** A price change that raises fuel profit but loses shop visits might not help as much as it looks.

**Wants the team to notice:** Remind them that fewer drivers also means fewer shop sales.

**Gets wrong:** She'd rather make small changes this quarter so the numbers don't jump.

### Dr. Kwame Osei, VP Political Risk

**Knows:** Germany's stations sit near the border, and drivers cross it for cheaper fuel.

**Believes:** Price cuts in Louisiana could look like a price war, and regulators notice price wars.

**Gets wrong:** He overstates how likely a regulator is to step in over a few cents a gallon.

### Margrethe Lund, Board member

**Knows:** The gas stations are where most customers ever meet Halden.

**Believes:** The stations matter to Halden's name, not just its profit.

**Wants the team to notice:** Ask them whether they're setting prices for this quarter or for the next two years, because drivers who leave tend not to come back quickly.

**Gets wrong:** She suggests putting electric chargers in the stations instead, which isn't a choice this quarter.

### Priya Venkatesan, Your chief of staff

**Knows:** Marcus Delacroix also runs the gas stations, and his bonus depends on Baton Rouge.

**Believes:** Predictions: if the team cuts prices, rivals will start matching within a quarter or two, and Danielle will call it a win before then. If the team raises prices in small towns, dealers will grumble but few will switch suppliers. Marcus won't object to anything that doesn't touch Baton Rouge.

**Gets wrong:** She expects rivals to match a cut immediately. It actually takes them a little while.

## Quarter 3 (Q3 2027): Rotterdam and Singapore

### Dr. Elena Marchetti, Chief Economist

**Knows:** WTI is up a little, to about $72.60. The European refining margin is near $4.90 a barrel, better than last winter but still thin. The Asian margin is about $12.50.

**Believes:** The question for Rotterdam isn't whether it makes a profit. It's whether it covers the costs that stop when it stops.

**Wants the team to notice:** Help them separate the costs that come with each barrel from the costs Rotterdam pays whether it runs or not.

**Gets wrong:** She'd like to see where European margins go next before deciding anything permanent.

### Danielle Roy, Head of Sales and Marketing

**Knows:** Halden's European stations can buy fuel from other refiners if Rotterdam stops.

**Believes:** The European stations will be fine either way.

### Bjørn Aasen, Head of Operations

**Knows:** Rotterdam earns the European refining margin plus $1.50 a barrel. It spends $4.10 a barrel on costs that come with each barrel it runs, and pays $2.30 a barrel of fixed costs on its full size whether it runs or not. Pausing stops the per-barrel costs but not the fixed ones, and adds $5 million a quarter of care costs. Restarting after a pause costs $85 million. Closing costs $150 million once and can't be undone. Singapore is run by Straits Pacific, which owns 60%; Halden can only ask how hard it runs.

**Believes:** Rotterdam covers its running costs and should keep going. Shutting it would throw away the most skilled crew in Europe.

**Wants the team to notice:** He'll walk through what Rotterdam earns and spends per barrel if asked.

**Gets wrong:** He'll talk up how hard and expensive a restart is, to make pausing sound worse than it is.

### Ana Ruiz, VP Finance

**Knows:** Closing Rotterdam costs $150 million once. Pausing costs $5 million a quarter in care costs on top of the fixed costs, and $85 million to restart.

**Believes:** Grant doesn't want a $150 million charge in a quarter where nothing forces it.

**Wants the team to notice:** Point out that one-time costs and running costs have to be compared over the same stretch of time.

**Gets wrong:** She leans toward whatever avoids a one-time charge this quarter.

### Dr. Kwame Osei, VP Political Risk

**Knows:** The Rotterdam works council has written to Erik Sandvik asking whether the refinery has a future before agreeing to the winter maintenance plan. Dutch law gives the works council a formal say on closures.

**Believes:** Closing Rotterdam would be read in Europe as Halden leaving Europe, and that has costs beyond the refinery.

**Gets wrong:** He'll suggest a closure could put the European stations at political risk, which overstates it.

### Margrethe Lund, Board member

**Knows:** Rotterdam has lost money in 7 of the last 10 quarters.

**Believes:** The board would rather hear a clear reason for whatever the team does with Rotterdam than see it drift.

**Wants the team to notice:** Ask them what would have to be true for Rotterdam to have a future.

**Gets wrong:** She floats turning Rotterdam into a biofuels plant, which isn't something the team can decide now.

### Priya Venkatesan, Your chief of staff

**Knows:** Straits Pacific owns 60% of Singapore and decides how hard it runs. The Rotterdam works council is waiting for an answer.

**Believes:** Predictions: Straits Pacific has never agreed to run Singapore below 80% or above 95% while I've been here; a request in that range will probably be accepted. If Rotterdam closes, Bjørn will take it personally and Marcus will be pleased. If Rotterdam pauses, the works council will ask when it restarts, every month.

**Gets wrong:** She thinks Marcus would be happy with a pause too. He'd rather see it closed.

## Quarter 4 (Q4 2027): the price Baton Rouge pays for Texas crude

### Dr. Elena Marchetti, Chief Economist

**Knows:** WTI is at $74 a barrel and traders expect it to stay about there. The European refining margin is about $4.60 a barrel. Texas crude sold on the open market goes for about 30 cents under WTI by the time it reaches Baton Rouge.

**Believes:** An internal price is only a way of keeping score between parts of the same company. It's worth asking what each choice makes people do.

**Wants the team to notice:** Help them see that the internal price moves profit between the oil fields, Baton Rouge and Geneva but doesn't change Halden's total by itself.

**Gets wrong:** She'd like a full study of how other oil companies set internal prices before deciding.

### Danielle Roy, Head of Sales and Marketing

**Believes:** This one's for finance and the traders.

### Bjørn Aasen, Head of Operations

**Knows:** Baton Rouge takes about 120,000 barrels a day of Halden's own Texas crude. It costs $14.10 a barrel to pump and ship that crude to Baton Rouge, plus a capital charge of $4.60, so the cost-based price is about $18.70.

**Believes:** Whatever the price, the same barrels flow through the same pipes. Operations don't change.

### Ana Ruiz, VP Finance

**Knows:** When Halden's own crude price is different from the outside market price, Geneva can trade on the gap. It keeps 35 cents of every dollar of difference, on up to 40,000 barrels a day. Geneva earns nothing from the gap when the internal price is within about 10% of the cost-based price or the market price, or at or above the market price. Marcus's bonus depends on Baton Rouge's profit; the oil fields team's on the oil fields' profit.

**Believes:** Grant cares that every part of Halden reports a profit people can trust. A price that hands Geneva easy money makes Geneva's numbers look better than its trading.

**Wants the team to notice:** Point them at who gains and who loses under each choice, and what each person will then do.

**Gets wrong:** She'll favour whatever price makes the quarter look smoothest across all three businesses.

### Dr. Kwame Osei, VP Political Risk

**Knows:** Tax authorities in the US and Europe look closely at prices one part of a company charges another, especially across borders.

**Believes:** A price that's hard to explain to a tax authority is a risk, even if it's legal.

**Gets wrong:** He overstates the tax risk of a price set at cost for a sale inside the US.

### Margrethe Lund, Board member

**Knows:** Lorenz Kuhn's Geneva desk just had its best quarter in four years.

**Believes:** The board wants to know Halden is run as one company, not as three businesses keeping score against each other.

**Wants the team to notice:** Ask them whether people at Halden should be paid on their own piece or on the whole.

### Priya Venkatesan, Your chief of staff

**Knows:** Marcus Delacroix wants Baton Rouge to pay what the crude costs to pump and ship. The oil fields team wants the market price. Lorenz Kuhn says he's fine either way. Grant Whitaker is worried about that.

**Believes:** Predictions: if the price goes to cost, Marcus will be delighted and the oil fields team will complain to Ingrid within a week. If it stays at market, Marcus will escalate to Sandvik. If it lands somewhere in between, Lorenz will be the happiest person in the building, and Grant will notice.

**Gets wrong:** She thinks Lorenz doesn't care about the price at all. He does.
