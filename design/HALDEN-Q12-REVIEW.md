# Quarter 12 review: what should Halden become?

**For Vikram, before merging PR #16.** Quarter 12 (Q4 2029) is the transition portfolio from the Week 12 package: the four-bucket, five-year plan that replaces the 2028 project list. This note has everything a student reads, what each advisor knows, the economics as built, the Quarter 11 bug this PR fixes, and the calls to confirm. ## 1. The quarter in one paragraph

The board gives Halden $1,800M over five years. $600M must keep what it has running; $1,200M is free to place, once. Five things are on the table: expand in the Permian ($650M), Helix at Rotterdam ($1,200M), convert part of Rotterdam to biofuels ($480M), offshore wind off Norway ($550M), and selling the 1,100 European stations (+$550M, which widens the envelope). The board caps each group (more oil $800M, next to what we do $1,200M, clean energy $700M), so Helix and biofuels can't both go, and the full transition bet (Helix plus wind, $1,750M) is affordable only with the sale. Each project is worth money in some futures and loses it in others, across three carbon prices ($40, $95, $180) and three paths for oil demand. There is no set that wins everywhere; the decision is a defended bet.

## 7. Industry news

**A price on carbon, at last.** The carbon price that Europe has had for years is spreading. At $40 a tonne it barely registers in a refinery's costs. At $180 it would reshape every oil company's plans. Analysts' five-year forecasts run from one end of that range to the other, which tells you how much anybody knows.

**Oil companies pick sides.** Three of Halden's rivals announced five-year plans this quarter. One is doubling down on shale. One is building wind farms and selling refineries. One is doing a little of each and being called unfocused by the analysts who called the other two reckless.

**Who wants gas stations in Europe.** Buyers have appeared for European station networks: convenience retailers and charging companies that want the sites more than the fuel. Prices paid this year have been about two years of a network's earnings, and sellers have taken them.
