## 1. The quarter in one paragraph

The Kessana government asks for 74% of the field's profit oil instead of 62%. Halden's $2.8B of development capital is in the ground, the field has 340M barrels left (ten years), and handing the block back fetches $180M. Staying beats leaving at every take on the grid; on economics alone the government could push to 98.5% before Halden walks. What limits the squeeze is what the government needs next: a result to announce before an election, the next phase of the field, its standing with the companies watching. The team answers once, on the Oil fields page, and the terms run for the rest of the course.

## 10. Calls to confirm

1. **Outcomes are deterministic.** Counter with reserves → 68%; threaten → 80%; sign → 74%; leave → $180M. No chance draw. If you'd rather the counter carried a risk (say 70% settle / 30% harsh, drawn per class like OPEC), it's a small change and I'd preset it on the quarter the same way.
2. **Volume: 150,000 bbl/day in the P&L, 34M bbl/year in the valuation.** The operating model has run Kessana at 150,000 bbl/day since Quarter 1 (about 55M bbl/year). The Week 11 package values the field at 340M barrels over ten years, level. Changing the model's volume would restate every quarter back to 2026, so I kept both and had the rule box and Marchetti say which is which: the valuation is a planning view at long-run Brent; the P&L is this quarter's production at this quarter's price. The package's "reserve-estimate quality" differentiator has no source in the v2 build (Quarter 1 never asked for a reserve estimate), so it's narrative: Halden's conservative 340M estimate is what makes the counter credible.
3. **Minister Tetteh has no first name.** The old build's counterparty list had only "Tetteh". I've used "Minister Tetteh" everywhere. Give me a first name if you want one.
4. **An unanswered team signs at 74%.** The alternative (nothing happens if you don't answer) would reward silence.
5. **Long-run Brent $78.50 vs spot $72.50.** The package's valuation price. The Q3 2029 market row has oil at $68 (Brent $72.50) after the recession quarter's $62. The exhibit states the planning price explicitly.
6. **Tetteh's standing feeds Quarter 14.** The design's Round 14 judges include Kessana's government "depending on the team's path"; the relations line set here is what that judge will read. Nothing else in Q12 or Q13 depends on the Kessana answer except the P&L.
