# Quarter 14 review: the board meets

**For Vikram, before merging PR #18.** Quarter 14 (Q2 2030) is the board defense. It is the one quarter that is not an operating round, so this note is longer on design calls than the others. There is no Week 14 package (by design: the handoff declared Week 14 an assessment week with no data), so the economics here is only the two draws and the valuation of the five-year plan; everything else is the defense, the judges and the verdict. Local play-through done with three demo teams.

## 1. The quarter in one paragraph

Nothing is set. Every page carries last quarter's settings, the engine runs Q2 2030 as usual, and the scorecard completes fourteen quarters. When the quarter opens, the world the five-year plan is judged in (a carbon price and a demand path) is drawn once for the class; at the close, whether a plant run past due broke down is drawn once for the class. Students write the board defense in three parts, with their day-one sentence and the drawn world beside it, and can read their whole record. When results go out, "What happened" is told by three judges. The instructor then reads the defense, decides whether the reasoning was strong, and publishes one of the four endings from the opening.

## 3. What's happening (briefing)

**Q2 2030 · Monday 1 April · Houston · The board meets in June**

Fourteen quarters ago the board gave you a job that didn't exist and one sentence to go with it: run Halden as one company, and show the board the job was worth creating. In June it decides. Erik has asked for your case in writing before the meeting, and Margrethe has told you, kindly, that the board has read every one of your memos and will ask about them.

There's nothing to set this quarter. Every page carries what you set last quarter, the plant runs, the stations sell fuel, and the numbers come in as they always have. If you put Baton Rouge's turnaround off, the crews arrive this quarter, and you'll find out whether the plant held. The five-year plan you placed in October gets its first real test: the world has started to show which way it's going.

The board will ask three things. Did your decisions add up to a company, or were they fourteen separate answers? Were they sound choices at the time, with what you knew then, whatever happened next? And do you understand your own run: what you got right, what you got wrong, and which was which?

**The big question:** Was this job worth creating, and what did you learn running it?

**How it works (rules box):** No decisions this quarter. Every page carries last quarter's settings, and the quarter runs as usual at the close: your fourteenth set of results, and your final score and rank against the class. Two things are decided for your class: if any team put off Baton Rouge's turnaround, whether the plant held is drawn at the close; and the world your five-year plan is valued in (a carbon price and a path for oil demand) is drawn when the quarter opens and shown on the Your board defense page. What you submit instead of a memo is your board defense, in three parts: the case for the company you ran, three of your own decisions defended against what you wrote at the time, and what you'd do differently. Your instructor reads it with your fourteen memos and your results, decides whether your reasoning was strong, and the board's verdict follows from that and from your results against the class: four endings, as you were told on your first day.

**Marchetti's line:** The last quarter's prices barely matter now. What matters is the world that's arriving: look at the carbon price and at what the analysts are saying about demand, because your five-year plan is about to be judged in one future, not nine. Then stop looking at prices and write the case.

## 5. The verdict

On the faculty feedback page for Quarter 14, above the defense and the usual AI drafts:

- **Reasoning:** Strong / Weak, the instructor's call from the defense and the memos.
- **Outcomes:** computed: the team's average score over the course against the class median ("strong (average 59.5 against a class median of 53.0)").
- **Ending:** the four-tier table suggests one; the instructor can pick another. Strong reasoning and strong outcomes → _They keep the job and give you more._ Weak reasoning, strong outcomes → _They keep the job, with conditions._ Strong reasoning, weaker outcomes → _They split the job back into three._ Weak and weaker → _They sell the European business without asking you._
- **Publish the verdict to the team**, enabled once the quarter's results are shown. Students then see the ending on The board's verdict page: the title from the opening, two paragraphs on the meeting, and a last line from Margrethe (or, for the sale, "Margrethe did not catch you on the way out.").

The faculty board's Memo column becomes "Board defense" in Quarter 14 and shows "Defense saved / No defense yet" and "Verdict sent / decided, not sent / none".

## 8. Calls to confirm

1. **The verdict is the instructor's, half computed.** Reasoning is your call (strong/weak); outcomes come from the course-average score against the class median (so in a class of three, two teams are "strong"). You can override the ending before publishing. If you'd rather set both halves by hand, the outcomes button is a small change.
2. **The ending mapping.** Strong reasoning with weaker outcomes splits the job; weak reasoning with strong outcomes keeps it with conditions. The opening's second card ("Your results are fine, but you haven't convinced them why") matches the latter; I read the third card ("one person can't run all of it") as the former. Say so if you'd swap them.
3. **The world is drawn, not chosen.** Odds are mine (the package gave none): carbon 30/45/25, demand 45/35/20. You can preset it on the quarter; a faculty button for presets (world, OPEC, breakdown) is a sensible small follow-up.
4. **The defense is mirrored into the memo.** So the existing AI drafts (feedback, writing score, mismatch check) read the whole defense; the mismatch check against "what they set" is less meaningful in Quarter 14 and the drafter's prompt doesn't know it's a defense. A Q14-specific feedback prompt (the three rubric dimensions: coherence, decision quality, self-understanding) is the natural next piece if you want AI help with the tier call; I kept the AI out of the verdict itself.
5. **No live defense scheduling or presentation.** The handoff says the board defense is also a live presentation; the sim holds the written submission and the verdict. The live part is yours in class.
6. **The record page shows memos in full, collapsed.** Faculty feedback already published on earlier quarters isn't repeated there; students can open each quarter from the strip.
